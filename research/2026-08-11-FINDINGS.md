# Overnight Findings

Sixteen designs, ~330 agents, one 160-statement corpus. This file is the summary;
per-experiment detail is in the sibling research files.

## The result that reframes the rest

Arm E — the best-scoring design — was run twice. Identical codebook, identical
items, identical first four rotation offsets, identical model.

| run | raters | fine α |
|---|---|---|
| first | 4 | **0.898** |
| replication | 8 | **0.844** |

To test whether rater *count* explained the gap, all 70 four-rater subsets of the
replication were scored:

| | |
|---|---|
| min | 0.824 |
| median | **0.844** |
| max | 0.864 |
| **zero of 70 subsets reach 0.898** | |

Four raters from the replication agree exactly as much as eight do. The gap is
**between-run variance in the raters themselves, ≈0.05**.

**Consequence.** Every effect measured tonight below ~0.05 is inside the noise of
running the same design twice:

| effect | size | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 19 → 35 pairwise rules | +0.023 | not established |
| sharpened definitions | +0.018 | not established |
| `scope` field | +0.012 | not established |
| tiering (cascade-3 over flat) | +0.007 | not established |
| rich vs thin coarse book | −0.022 | not established |
| combined arm E over baseline | +0.045 | marginal |
| boolean battery (§4.1 mechanism) | **−0.092** | **real** |
| funnel-3, three tiers | **−0.110** | **real** |
| funnel-2, three tiers | **−0.172** | **real** |

**Large failures survived. Most apparent wins did not.** A single four-rater run
is not enough to distinguish designs that differ by two or three points.

`principle` was reported at α 0.962 from a four-rater arm — twice, in this
session, as evidence that the target was reachable per-label. With eight raters
it measures **0.784**. That was noise, and there was a warning sign one run
earlier: `assumption`, whose definition did not change between two arms, moved
1.000 → 0.220.

## Best current estimate

**α ≈ 0.84 fine, ≈ 0.88 coarse**, eight raters, fifteen labels, on a corpus
spanning quant research, RFCs, reference documentation, postmortems, onboarding
material and three chat sources.

For scale: the argumentative-zoning scheme reached κ 0.71 with seven categories
and a 111-page codebook; CoreSC reached 0.50–0.57 with eleven.

## What was ruled out, with measurements

**Fewer labels.** The best single merge — `observation` + `principle`, the largest
collision in the project — buys **+0.007**. Dropping `observation` entirely (386
assignments) moves α by +0.001. Collapsing 15 labels to 9 reaches 0.927 on
four-rater data and destroys the taxonomy's usefulness.

**Splitting compound statements.** Compound sentences score *higher* than simple
ones (0.881 vs 0.871); 45+ word sentences score 0.943 against 0.850 for 20–30
word ones. More text means more surface cues. Splitting would remove them.

**Regrouping the coarse tier.** Three families is the optimum asked directly
(0.918), better than five (0.902) or two (0.874). A two-way `practice`/`claim`
cut scored 0.948 *derived* from roll-up statistics and 0.874 *asked directly* —
groupings optimised on roll-ups do not transfer.

**Tiering.** Worth +0.007 with a weak codebook and **−0.026** with a good one:
the two levers are substitutes, not complements. A cascade rations the codebook
across stages, and the rationing costs more than the narrower option set saves.

**Interior tiers.** Strictly harmful. Both three-tier funnels put the majority of
their errors in the middle tier (25 and 39 of ~50). A tier only pays for itself
if it beats the decision it replaces, and an abstract 1-of-3 does not beat a
concrete 1-of-7. **Concreteness beats narrowness.**

**The spec's own §4.1 mechanism.** Independent booleans plus priority resolution:
−0.092, and the §4.1 priority order is worse than resolving alphabetically. Per-
test agreement is fine; the loss is entirely in recombination. Retained only for
multi-label output — 38% of statements fire two or more tests, and asked
separately raters say yes to both.

## The one durable qualitative finding

Of the seven changes that failed, six were **elaborations that made the rater
reason more**: judge whether a claim generalises; decide whether an authority
exists; consider what a family excludes; sort into invented mid-level groups;
recombine fifteen booleans; expect most tests not to fire.

The two things that helped both **reduce** reasoning: state the pairwise rule
outright, or name the literal wording to look for.

**Agreement comes from pointing, not from thinking.** Every time the rater was
given more to consider, they considered it differently from one another.

With the caveat that surface anchoring is a **repair, not an improvement** —
applied to all fifteen labels it *lost* 0.041, because naming the words helps a
vague label and handcuffs a crisp one. `prohibition` fell 0.958 → 0.594 when
given a word list narrower than the model's own grasp of the concept.

## What should change in the spec

1. `[MEASURED]` Replace the headline reliability figure with **α 0.844 fine /
   0.878 coarse, eight raters**, and mark all four-rater figures as provisional.
2. `[MEASURED]` §4.1's boolean-battery mechanism must not be presented as the
   design. Keep it for multi-label output with the −0.092 attached.
3. `[MEASURED]` §4.1's priority order is worse than alphabetical. If retained,
   "most reliable test wins" is the best of six rules tried.
4. Adopt the 35-rule pairwise set and the sharpened `principle`/`distinction`
   definitions — marginal individually, but the combined +0.045 is the largest
   effect that is not clearly noise, and neither has a downside.
5. Add `scope` (`instance`/`system`/`world`): 0.904 with eight raters, the most
   reliable field in the system.
6. Target `architecture` (0.576), `procedure` (0.632) and `background` (0.652).
   These are now measured with enough data to be worth working on.

## Methodological rule this session earned

**One four-rater run cannot distinguish designs.** Every future comparison needs
either eight raters or two independent runs per arm, and any effect under 0.05
should be reported as unresolved rather than as a result.

I did not follow this rule tonight. Fourteen designs were ranked on single
four-rater runs, and the ranking is not trustworthy below ~0.05. The failures are
robust; the ordering of the top six is not.
